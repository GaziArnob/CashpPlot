CashPilot: an AI financial assistant for small and medium businesses · Oct 20, 2026 · @Arnob
This document describes what is built and running. Items not built yet are marked planned.

Introduction

CashPilot is an AI financial assistant for small and medium businesses. It warns owners about cash shortages 7 to 30 days in advance, tells them who to collect money from first, builds an action plan to close the gap, gives them an explainable credit score, and estimates how much they can safely borrow. Live: https://cashpilot-ai-demo.vercel.app · Source: https://github.com/GaziArnob/CashpPlot (branch cashpilot-final).

1. System Architecture

The system has four layers: a Next.js web client, a FastAPI application server, an intelligence layer of deterministic engines plus an AI agent, and a PostgreSQL data layer. Requests flow top-down; the agent reaches data only by calling the engines as tools.

1.1 Design principle: deterministic core, AI interface

Every financial number is computed by deterministic, testable engines. The LLM never calculates figures; it interprets the user's intent, picks which engine to call, and explains the result. Any number in an AI answer that the engines did not produce is detected and the answer is replaced by a template. This keeps every output auditable and prevents hallucinated numbers.

No paid AI services. All AI features run on free options behind one LLM provider layer; the provider order is one setting (LLM_PROVIDERS=gemini,groq,ollama). If a free-tier rate limit is hit or a provider fails, the request falls back to the next one, and finally to templates, so the product works with no AI key at all.

1.6 Request flow example

A user asks: "Will I run short on cash next week?"

  1. The client sends POST /agent/chat.
  2. The Agent Service sends the question and the tool schemas to the LLM.
  3. The LLM requests get_cash_forecast(days=7).
  4. The Agent Service calls the Forecast Engine with the business_id from the session; the engine reads transactions, invoices and bills.
  5. The engine returns a shortage date and gap amount.
  6. The Agent Service passes the result back to the LLM.
  7. The LLM writes the answer, for example: "You may be short about 12,000 on Oct 22. Rahim Store owes you 15,000, 10 days overdue; collecting it covers the gap."
  8. Any figure in the answer is checked against the tool results, and the answer streams back to the client.

2. Core Algorithm Description

Several engines do the analysis. Each is deterministic and testable, and the AI agent only calls them as tools.

2.1 Cash flow prediction

Goal: project the daily cash balance for the next H days (H = 7 to 30) and flag shortages.

  1. Daily series. Group transactions by day into income I(t) and expense E(t), over the last 180 days. Scheduled items (rent, salaries, loan installments, bills) are removed from the trend model and placed on their exact day instead, because a few large monthly payments distort a daily trend. Outliers are capped.
  2. Baseline forecast. Holt-Winters additive smoothing with a damped trend and weekly seasonality (period 7). With less than 60 days of history, fall back to a 28-day weighted moving average multiplied by a day-of-week factor (average of each weekday over the last 12 weeks).
  3. Known future events. Add expected invoice inflows, weighted by each customer's historical on-time payment rate, and subtract bills, loan installments and recurring payables on their due dates. Each amount is counted once and tagged confirmed (known obligations) or estimated (forecast sales and running costs).
  4. Balance projection. Roll the balance forward day by day.
  5. Shortage detection. Flag every day where the projected balance falls below the business's cash threshold.
Î(t) = WMA₂₈(I) · DOW_I(t), Ê(t) = WMA₂₈(E) · DOW_E(t)
B(t) = B(t−1) + Î(t) − Ê(t) + Σinvoices due t aᵢ · p_collect(cᵢ) − Σbills due t bⱼ
Shortage at t ⇔ B(t) < threshold, Gap(t) = threshold − B(t)

An 80% confidence band (±1.28 times the standard deviation of past one-step forecast errors) is drawn around the line. A day is marked "at risk" when only the lower edge of the band crosses the threshold. Output: daily projected balance, shortage dates, gap amounts and the main drivers of each shortage.

2.2 Smart payment recovery

Goal: rank customers by who should be contacted first, suggest what to do, set a credit limit, and draft a reminder.

Priority = 100 · (0.30·A + 0.25·D + 0.15·R + 0.15·C + 0.15·P)
FeatureDefinition (normalized 0 to 1)Weight
AAmount owed, log-scaled against the largest balance0.30
DAging bucket weight: current 0.05, 0-30 days 0.35, 31-60 days 0.60, 61-90 days 0.85, 90+ days 1.000.25
RPayment-history risk = 1 − on-time rate (new customers 0.5)0.15
CHow much of the forecast cash gap the overdue amount covers0.15
PProbability of a payment within 7 days: a logistic regression trained on this business's own past invoices when there are at least 40 examples and 10 positives, otherwise transparent rules0.15

The score maps to a suggested action (stop credit and visit, visit, call, offer a 2% early-payment discount, send a reminder, or wait) and to a reminder tone (friendly, firm or final) that follows how late the invoice is and how risky the customer is. A new customer gets a starting credit limit. The LLM fills a template with only the customer's name, amount, due date and tone; it sees no other customer data, and its output is checked against the template's figures.

2.3 AI credit scoring (explainable)

Goal: a score from 300 to 900 with transparent reasons, computed over the last 6 to 12 months.

Explainability. Each factor's contribution is measured against a baseline bᵢ, the value for a typical healthy business. The largest positive and negative contributions are shown as reasons, for example: "High outstanding receivables lowered your score by 38 points." A new account with little data is labelled "only a starting point" and told exactly which data to add, and the confidence of the score is shown.

How to raise it. The planner re-runs the same formula for each possible action (for example collecting a specific overdue invoice, or paying the next bills on time) and reports the points each would earn and the steps to the next band and the next loan type. Nothing is guessed.

Planned: once real repayment outcomes are available, a gradient-boosted model could replace the fixed weights, with SHAP values keeping each score explainable.

2.4 Loan readiness and recommendation

Goal: estimate a safe loan amount and matching products. The output is an estimate, not a loan approval.

  1. Conservative capacity. Take the 25th percentile of monthly net cash flow over the last 12 months (NCFp25), so one strong month cannot inflate capacity.
  2. Safe monthly installment. Take a share α of that, set by credit band, minus existing installments: α = 0.40 (Excellent), 0.35 (Good), 0.25 (Fair), 0.15 (Needs improvement).
  3. Maximum principal. Invert the standard EMI formula, with r the monthly interest rate and n the tenure in months.
  4. Product matching. The loan catalog has 8 loan types with minimum scores from 500 to 700 and different rates. Keep the products where the business's score meets min_score and Pmax covers min_amount; rank by total cost of credit, lowest first. Each type is shown with a status such as available, too small to repay, or score too low.
  5. Readiness checklist. Rule-based actions that would raise the score or capacity, such as "Collect receivables older than 30 days".
SafeEMI = α · NCFp25 − ExistingEMI
Pmax = SafeEMI · ((1+r)ⁿ − 1) / (r (1+r)ⁿ)

Owners can record loans they already have (My loans); their installments then count in the cash forecast and in the credit score. Introduction requests to a lender need the owner's consent and are only recorded and tracked; nothing is sent to a lender in this version.

2.5 What-if simulator

The user changes inputs, such as a new loan, a sales change of x%, or a new fixed expense, and the Forecast Engine re-runs with them. The original and scenario balance curves are shown side by side.

Î'(t) = Î(t)(1 + x), Ê'(t) = Ê(t) + NewExpense + NewEMI

2.6 Insight engine

Rule-based checks compare each expense category's last 30 days with its monthly average over the 90 days before. A change over 20% raises an insight, which the LLM phrases for the dashboard summary. The same engine produces the monthly profit-and-loss report.

2.7 Cash Advisor (action plan)

When the forecast shows a shortage, the planner orders the cheapest and safest steps first: (1) collect overdue money from the highest-scoring customers, (2) clear slow and dead stock at a discount, (3) ask suppliers to delay bills that fall before the gap, (4) cut owner drawings and spending that jumped, (5) scale up the star products once there is spare cash, and (6) financing, only for what is still missing after steps 1 to 4. Each step is applied to the forecast to measure its real effect, and the plan shows how much of the gap it covers. The owner marks steps done, dismisses or reopens them; nothing executes by itself.

2.8 Supplier payment plans

The payables engine builds a dated 30-day cash flow in which every amount is counted exactly once and tagged confirmed or estimated, finds days where payments clash with low cash, and proposes steps (instalments, moving a bill, short-term financing) that actually lift the lowest balance above the safety level. A draft message to the supplier is written from a template with the supplier's name masked; the owner approves the plan and records the supplier's reply. Financing offers shown are demo data.

2.9 Product performance

For each product over the last 30 and 90 days: units, revenue and growth, gross margin, profit contribution, inventory turnover, days of stock left, and cash conversion speed (cash versus credit sales and how long credit takes to come back). Products are classed as star, cash cow, slow mover, dead stock or new, and each gets a recommendation (push, keep, shrink, clear).

2.10 AI financial agent

The agent runs a bounded tool-use loop of at most 5 steps. Its tools are get_cash_forecast, get_overdue_customers, get_credit_score, get_loan_recommendation, run_what_if, get_spending_insights and draft_reminder.

  1. Send the system prompt, the user's question and the tool schemas to the LLM.
  2. If the LLM requests a tool, validate the arguments, run the engine with the business_id from the authenticated session, and return the result to the LLM.
  3. Repeat until the LLM answers in text, or stop at 5 steps with a fallback message.

Three grounding rules apply: every number in an answer must come from a tool result; the model can never choose the business_id; and all tools are read-only, so any action such as sending a reminder needs the user's confirmation in the UI. If no LLM provider is reachable, the agent falls back to keyword intents and template answers, so the chat always works.

2.11 Alerts and the daily job

Sixteen rules (cash shortage, cash already low, overdue customers, late and due invoices, bills due, supplier plan payments, loan installments, low stock, credit score drops, spending spikes, sales drops, lender follow-ups, goal deadlines, bank reminders, plan and trial reminders) create notifications with a severity and a link to the page that fixes the problem. Each rule runs in isolation, so one failure does not stop the others, and a dedupe key plus cooldown prevents repeats. A daily job refreshes score snapshots and action plans and can email urgent alerts once per day.

3. Security Design

Every request is authenticated, every query is scoped to one business, contact details are encrypted at field level, and the AI agent can only read data through scoped, read-only tools.

3.1 Authentication

Password storagebcrypt, cost factor 12
Access tokenJWT (HS256), 15-minute lifetime
Refresh tokenRotating, 7-day lifetime, stored server-side by id so it can be revoked, in an httpOnly, secure, SameSite=Strict cookie
Brute-force protectionRate limits per client address and signed-in user (10 auth requests per minute); the account locks for 15 minutes after 5 failed logins
Second factorOptional authenticator-app code (TOTP, 6 digits, 30 seconds). SMS codes are not used.

3.2 Authorization and tenant isolation

  • business_id scoping. Every business-owned record has a business_id. Every query filters by the business of the authenticated user, never by one sent in the request.
  • Row-Level Security. PostgreSQL RLS policies (forced, with the business set per request) enforce the same rule in the database as a second line of defence. The database role must not be a superuser. Seed, background jobs and the lender view run with an explicit bypass flag.
  • Object-level checks. Any ID in a URL, such as /invoices/{id}, is checked for ownership before it is read or changed.
  • Roles. owner (full access), staff (transactions, ledger and customers only), lender (read-only, consent-scoped). Premium features are additionally gated by plan.

3.4 Consent-based lender access

Lenders see a business's score and reports only after the owner grants access. Each grant names its scope (which data), has an expiry date and can be revoked at any time. The token is shown once and stored hashed. Every lender view is written to the audit log.

3.5 AI-specific security

Free-tier providers may keep or review submitted data, so only minimal fields are sent to them: amounts, dates and category names, with customer and supplier names replaced by codes, and never phone numbers, emails or account details. Owners choose cloud, local-only or no AI in Settings; in local-only mode only a model on the server is used.

RiskMitigation
Prompt injection through user data (a transaction note or receipt text)Tool results and receipt text are passed as data, and the prompts tell the model to ignore any instructions inside them
Cross-tenant data leakTools take business_id from the server session; the model cannot choose which business to query
Unauthorized actionsAll agent tools are read-only; side effects such as sending a reminder need explicit user confirmation
Hallucinated figuresNumbers come only from engine outputs, and any figure in an answer that is not in the tool results causes the answer to be replaced by a template
Over-sharing with the LLMOnly the fields a tool needs are sent; no passwords, tokens or contact lists
Runaway loops and cost abuseAt most 5 tool steps per request, a request timeout, and per-user AI rate limits (20 per minute)

3.6 Application security

  • Input validation: Pydantic schemas on every endpoint, with type, range and length checks.
  • SQL injection: ORM with parameterized queries only.
  • Cross-site scripting: React output escaping; the API replies with Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'.
  • CSRF: the refresh cookie is SameSite=Strict, and the cookie-authenticated route also requires a custom X-Requested-With header that a cross-site form cannot set.
  • File uploads: type and size limits (CSV up to 2 MB; JPEG, PNG or WebP images up to 5 MB); files are processed in memory.
  • Rate limiting: per client address and signed-in user on auth (10 per minute), AI (20 per minute) and upload (10 per minute) endpoints. The limiter is in memory; it would move to Redis for several workers.
  • Security headers: HSTS, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: DENY, Referrer-Policy: no-referrer, Cache-Control: no-store.
  • Messaging: reminders are sent only when the owner presses Send; subject lines are protected against header injection; a failed send keeps the draft and shows the provider's reason.

3.7 Monitoring and audit

The audit log records signups, logins (including failed ones), onboarding, data exports, lender access grants, views and revocations, reminders sent, payment plan approvals, bank connections, staff changes, two-factor changes, plan changes and settings changes. Application logs never contain passwords, tokens or full financial records. Planned: alerts on logins from unusual locations and on bulk data access.

3.8 Responsible AI and compliance

  • Every credit score shows the factors behind it.
  • Loan output is labelled "Estimate only, not a loan approval."
  • Introduction to a lender needs an explicit consent tick.
  • Users can export or delete their data at any time (account deletion also cancels a subscription first).
  • The design follows data-minimization and consent principles in line with GDPR-style practice and applicable local data-protection rules. We do not claim certifications or a penetration test.
